# Red Bull — "No vendió una bebida. Construyó un mundo." — scene spec (shared by the 3D and 2D versions)

Vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, 96 s. Spanish narration is already on the timeline (assets/narration.wav).
Central idea: Red Bull didn't build a beverage brand; it built a world people wanted to belong to.
Tone: premium cinematic documentary about business strategy — Apple product film + sports documentary.
Never an energy-drink ad. Dark cinematic environments mixed with energetic outdoor scenes, high contrast,
subtle particles / atmosphere, dynamic camera. Visual storytelling over text. Text only where noted.

Brand rules: no real logos. The can is a slim aluminium can, two-tone deep blue / silver with a diamond
checker seam and an abstract warm sun disk (kit.makeCan in the 3D version). Never draw a bull, never write
"Red Bull" on the can, never draw Coca-Cola/Pepsi logos — use archetypes (red cola cans, dark cola bottles,
blue cans, lemon-lime green bottles).

Times below are GLOBAL seconds; "local" = global − scene start. Narration line windows are exact.

## Scene briefs

1. **Hook.** Extreme macro of the can rotating slowly in darkness, condensation on aluminium, camera creeping
   closer. Around 5.8 the can suddenly becomes tiny against an enormous world forming behind it — mountains,
   a racetrack, aircraft, crowd lights. The can stays in the foreground. At "identidad" (≈8.7) the whole
   environment transforms into a massive universe surrounding the product (light bursts outward, the world
   locks in).
2. **Competition.** 1980s supermarket: towering shelves of giant traditional soft drinks (red cola cans,
   dark bottles, green lemon-lime) dominating the frame. One small two-tone can appears among them. Slow push
   toward the small can. Feeling: tiny newcomer, market of giants.
3. **Strategy.** A dim war room; a huge wall covered with ads, billboards, consumer charts, sports clippings.
   A businessman silhouette studies it, then the traditional ad concepts are pulled off the wall one by one
   until the room is empty. He pins one single image: an extreme athlete mid-air. The visual language shifts
   (cold → warm light) as the second line plays.
4. **Discovery.** A lone athlete on a mountain ridge, wind in the clothes, camera orbiting slowly. Then the
   scene expands: more athletes appear across the landscape — snowboarders, motocross riders, climbers,
   surfers. The can is NOT visible. A lifestyle, not a product.
5. **Extreme world.** Fast intelligent montage, each cut uses the motion of the previous shot:
   motocross launch over a massive jump (at 37.7) → wheel spin matches an aircraft turbine → snowboard trick
   (39.0) with a rotation that matches a racing wheel → aircraft flying through mountain canyons (40.3) →
   formula car entering a corner at extreme speed (41.6). Before 37.7 (32.1–37.1) build the entry into this
   world (a door of light opening onto the extreme world, or a dive into it).
6. **Becomes content.** A massive futuristic media control room. Screens show sports, athletes, racing,
   adventure. The camera flies through the screens; the screens multiply into a vast wall/tunnel. During the
   second line show events, teams, media production, athletes, live broadcasts (LIVE dots, timecodes, camera
   feeds). It should feel like a media company.
7. **Transformation.** The can sits on a table. The camera pulls back and the room expands into a racing
   circuit, a stadium, a mountain, a media studio, an aircraft hangar, a massive audience. By 57.0 the can is
   a tiny element inside an enormous ecosystem.
8. **Emotional association.** At 60.9 rapid montage sharing one visual identity: athlete about to jump, crowd
   screaming, race car accelerating, skydiver falling, mountain landscape, slow-motion landing. At 65.0
   everything STOPS — black, silence. At 65.9 the can appears alone in the centre.
9. **The world.** The can transforms into a glowing abstract energy source; that energy spreads outward and
   builds the world of the film (sports, people, events, adventure). The brand as the source of the world.
10. **The lesson.** Everything disappears: a completely dark minimal space, a single product in the centre
    (74.3). At 79.1 the can slowly fades into the background. At 82.5 the world begins forming around the
    product again. At 86.4 the world keeps growing. Final shot 89.5–93.5: massive cinematic world around the
    tiny can, slow camera pull-back (leave the centre band y≈700–1000 calm for the overlay typography).
